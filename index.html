What is Weather?

Short-Term Atmospheric State (Hours to Days)

Weather refers to the instantaneous or short-term state of the Earth's atmosphere at a specific time and location. It is highly dynamic and fluctuates within minutes, hours, or days. Key weather parameters include air temperature, precipitation, humidity, barometric pressure, wind speed, and cloud cover.

मौसम (Weather) किसी विशिष्ट समय और स्थान पर वायुमंडल की क्षणिक या अल्पकालिक स्थिति को दर्शाता है। यह अत्यधिक गतिशील है और मिनटों, घंटों या दिनों के भीतर तेजी से बदलता है। मौसम के प्रमुख मापदंडों में हवा का तापमान, वर्षा, आर्द्रता, वायुमंडलीय दबाव, हवा की गति और बादलों का आवरण शामिल हैं।

What is Climate?

Long-Term Weather Averages (30+ Years)

Climate is the long-term statistical aggregate of weather conditions, seasonal cycles, and extreme climate events recorded across a geographic region over an extended standard period (defined by WMO as 30 years or more). While weather changes daily, climate shifts slowly over multi-decade scales.

जलवायु (Climate) किसी भौगोलिक क्षेत्र में लंबी अवधि (WMO द्वारा परिभाषित 30 वर्ष या अधिक) के मौसम की स्थितियों, मौसमी चक्रों और जलवायु चरम सीमाओं का सांख्यिकीय औसत है। मौसम दैनिक रूप से बदलता है, जबकि जलवायु बहु-दशकीय समय पर धीरे-धीरे बदलती है।

Real-World Examples of Climate Zones in India:

  • Hot Arid Desert climate of Thar in Rajasthan with low annual rainfall (<300 mm).
  • Alpine Cold Desert climate of Leh & Ladakh in the Trans-Himalayas.
  • Tropical Monsoon climate along Western Ghats with rainfall exceeding 3,000 mm.
  • Subtropical Humid monsoonal climate of Indo-Gangetic Plains.
The 6 Core Drivers of India's Weather & Climate Systems

1. South-West Monsoon (SW Monsoon)

Operating from June to September, bringing ~75% of India's total annual rainfall through Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal branches.

2. Western Disturbances (WDs)

Extratropical storms originating over the Mediterranean Sea, bringing vital winter rain and mountain snowfall to North India.

3. El Niño & La Niña (ENSO Oscillation)

Pacific Ocean sea surface temperature shifts that modulate monsoonal strength; El Niño typically suppresses rain while La Niña enhances it.

4. Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD)

SST difference between Western and Eastern Indian Ocean. A positive IOD boosts Indian monsoon rainfall significantly.

5. Himalayan Orographic Barrier

Blocks frigid Siberian winter winds from entering the subcontinent while trapping monsoonal moisture clouds to produce intense rainfall.

6. Tropical Cyclonic Surges

Pre-monsoon and post-monsoon cyclonic storms formed in the Bay of Bengal and Arabian Sea affecting coastal states.
